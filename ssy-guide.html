SSY guide

How to Open an SSY Account in 2026?

A step-by-step overview of eligibility, documents, deposit rules and how the SSY estimate on GovtSave works.

What is Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana?

Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY) is a government-backed small savings scheme meant for the future education and marriage expenses of a girl child. The account is opened by a parent or legal guardian and offers a notified interest rate that is reviewed every quarter.

Key facts at a glance

  • Interest rate used in our calculator: p.a., compounded annually
  • Deposits are made for 15 years; the account matures at 21 years
  • Minimum ₹250 and maximum ₹1,50,000 deposit in a financial year

Who can open an account?

  • The girl child should generally be below 10 years of age at the time of opening.
  • A parent or legal guardian opens and operates the account until she reaches the permitted age.
  • Normally one account is allowed per girl child, and a family can open accounts for up to two girls (with exceptions for twins or triplets).

Documents usually required

  • Birth certificate of the girl child
  • Identity and address proof of the parent/guardian
  • Passport-size photographs and the account-opening form
  • Initial deposit (cash, cheque or as accepted by the branch)

Requirements can vary slightly between post offices and authorised banks, so confirm the current list before visiting.

How to open the account

  1. Choose an India Post office or an authorised bank branch that offers SSY.
  2. Collect and fill in the SSY account-opening form.
  3. Submit the form with documents and make the first deposit.
  4. Collect the passbook and note the maturity date and deposit deadlines.

How the GovtSave estimate works

Our calculator assumes one deposit at the start of every year for 15 years and compounds the balance annually until year 21 at the configured rate. It is a simplified model; official interest depends on the balance and credit rules of the scheme.

Withdrawals and tax

A limited partial withdrawal is permitted after the girl reaches the specified age for higher education or marriage, subject to the scheme rules. Under the old tax regime, deposits may qualify for a Section 80C deduction and the interest and maturity amount are generally tax-exempt. Tax law changes, so verify the current position.
