PPF guide

PPF Account: Rules, Benefits and Returns

How the Public Provident Fund works – deposits, tenure, extensions, withdrawals and what our estimate assumes.

What is a Public Provident Fund?

The PPF is a long-term, government-backed savings scheme popular for retirement and goal-based saving. The interest rate is notified by the government every quarter, and the account has a fixed 15-year term that can be extended in blocks of 5 years.

Key facts at a glance

  • Interest rate used in our calculator: p.a., compounded annually
  • Minimum ₹500 and maximum ₹1,50,000 per financial year
  • Tenure options in the calculator: 15, 20 and 25 years

Deposit rules

  • Deposits can be made in a lump sum or in instalments, in multiples of ₹50.
  • Interest is worked out on the lowest balance between the 5th and the last day of each month, so depositing before the 5th can help.
  • One PPF account per individual is allowed; joint accounts are not permitted.

Extension, loans and withdrawals

  • Extension: After 15 years you can extend in 5-year blocks, with or without fresh contributions.
  • Loan: Available against the balance during a specified window in the early years of the account.
  • Partial withdrawal: Allowed from the 7th financial year, subject to a limit.

Tax treatment

PPF is commonly described as EEE: contributions may qualify for a Section 80C deduction (old tax regime), interest is exempt and the maturity amount is exempt. Confirm the latest rules for your situation.

How the GovtSave estimate works

The calculator assumes one deposit at the beginning of each year and compounds annually at a fixed rate. The official method uses monthly balances, so your actual maturity value can be slightly different.
